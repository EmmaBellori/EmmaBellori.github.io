How to Help Kids Handle Losing a Game

Five practical ways to help children manage disappointment after losing, calm down, and enjoy playing together again.

A practical guide for parents and caregivers

1. Prepare before the game

Agree that games can be fun even when we do not win. Decide together what to do if feelings get too big.

2. Pause and breathe

If frustration rises, stop playing for a moment. Invite a few slow breaths or a drink of water, without insisting that the child calm down instantly.

3. Name the feeling without judging

“You really wanted to win, and you feel upset.” Accepting the feeling does not mean accepting hurtful words or unsafe behavior.

4. Model good sportsmanship

Let children hear you say, “Good game,” and notice what was enjoyable. Show that adults can lose without blaming others.

5. Try again when ready

After a break, ask whether they want to replay, choose a different game, or stop for the day. Practice coping gradually rather than demanding instant perfection.

A quick game-time ritual

Before playing, agree on one kind phrase everyone can say at the end, such as “Thanks for playing.” After the game, invite each person to name one fun moment, regardless of who won. Keep the ritual brief and voluntary.

Words to use in the moment

Try “You wanted to win, and losing feels really hard.” Then offer a choice: “Would you like a short break or to sit with me?” Discuss fair play only after the upset has eased.

Frequently asked question

What if my child gets angry every time they lose?

Start with short, low-stakes games and predictable breaks. If reactions are intense, persistent, or affect daily life, consider asking a qualified professional for individualized guidance.

Every child is different. These general ideas are not a substitute for personalized professional support.
