10 Screen-Free Halloween Activities for Kids

Simple Halloween ideas for quiet time, family play and creative afternoons — no app, video or special kit required.

Easy ideas for parents, caregivers and teachers

1. Draw a silly monster

Take turns choosing one feature at a time: three eyes, tiny wings, striped socks or an enormous hat. The stranger the combination, the better.

2. Make a Halloween scavenger hunt

Choose five things to spot around the house or neighborhood, such as something orange, something shaped like a moon, a spider decoration, a pumpkin and something that glows.

3. Invent a friendly ghost story

Start with one sentence — “A tiny ghost knocked on the wrong door…” — then let each person add one sentence. Keep going until the story finds a funny ending.

4. Create pumpkin faces on paper

Draw several pumpkin outlines and give each one a different expression: surprised, sleepy, excited, worried or proud. Ask children what each pumpkin might be thinking.

5. Play Halloween matching pairs

Draw simple pairs of symbols on small cards — two bats, two pumpkins, two moons — turn them face down and play a homemade memory game.

6. Count a spooky collection

Use safe everyday objects such as blocks, paper shapes or large craft pieces. Sort them into small groups, count them and compare which group has more or fewer.

7. Design a mini maze

Draw a path from a little ghost to a friendly haunted house. Add a few dead ends, then swap mazes with someone else and try to solve them.

8. Make a pattern challenge

Create a repeating sequence with drawn symbols — pumpkin, bat, pumpkin, bat — and ask what comes next. Make the patterns longer as the game gets easier.

9. Try a Halloween drawing prompt

Combine two ordinary ideas: a witch at the grocery store, a vampire brushing his teeth, or a skeleton walking a tiny dog. Give everyone five minutes to draw their version.

10. Finish with a calm coloring break

After an active game, switch to a quieter activity such as coloring, tracing or a simple puzzle. It can be an easy way to transition toward dinner, bedtime or the next part of the day.

Keep it easy

You do not need to complete all ten activities. Pick one or two that fit the time, supplies and energy you have. For activities involving scissors or small craft pieces, use age-appropriate materials and adult supervision.
