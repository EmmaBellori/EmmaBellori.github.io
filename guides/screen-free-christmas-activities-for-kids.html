10 Screen-Free Christmas Activities for Kids

Simple holiday ideas for cozy afternoons, family time and quiet moments — no app, video or special kit required.

Easy ideas for parents, caregivers and teachers

1. Design a paper ornament

Draw a large circle, star or bell and decorate it with patterns, shapes and a name. Cut it out with adult help if needed and add it to the tree or a window.

2. Make a Christmas scavenger hunt

Look for five festive things around the house or neighborhood: something red, something shiny, a star, a bell and something shaped like a tree.

3. Invent a silly snowman

Draw a snowman with unexpected details — roller skates, a chef hat, three scarves or a tiny pet. Take turns choosing the next funny feature.

4. Tell a one-sentence Christmas story

Start with “On Christmas Eve, a tiny present began to wiggle…” and let each person add one sentence until the story reaches a happy ending.

5. Play holiday matching pairs

Draw simple pairs of symbols on cards — stars, mittens, candy canes, snowflakes — turn them face down and play a homemade memory game.

6. Count a festive collection

Use safe everyday objects, paper shapes or blocks. Sort them into groups, count each group and compare which has more, fewer or the same number.

7. Create a mini maze

Draw a path from a gingerbread friend to a Christmas tree. Add a few dead ends, then swap mazes and see who can find the route.

8. Make a repeating pattern

Try star, tree, star, tree — then ask what comes next. Add a third symbol when the first pattern becomes easy.

9. Write or draw a kindness idea

Think of one small kind thing to do during the holidays: help set the table, make a card, share a toy or thank someone. Draw the idea if writing feels like too much.

10. Finish with a calm coloring break

After an active game, switch to coloring, tracing or a simple puzzle. It can be an easy transition into dinner, bedtime or a quieter part of the day.

Keep it flexible

You do not need to complete all ten activities. Pick one or two that fit the time and materials you have. For activities involving scissors or small craft pieces, use age-appropriate materials and adult supervision.
